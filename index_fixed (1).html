What I Found
  • Furniture is generating a negative profit margin of 2.5%, even though it brings in decent sales. This tells me the discounts being given on furniture are way too high.
  • Technology is the most profitable category at 17.4% margin, but it is not being pushed as much as it should be.
  • I noticed that orders with no discount have an average margin of 34.0%, but once discounts go above 31%, the margin drops to -91.5%. That is a huge difference and shows discounting is being overused.
  • Out of 48 months, only 26 hit the 10% growth target. The business is fairly consistent but keeps falling slightly short.
  • Around 21% of customers fall in the "At Risk" segment, meaning they used to buy regularly but have gone quiet recently. A simple follow-up campaign could bring them back.
  • The Pareto analysis showed that a small group of products is driving most of the revenue, which means the business should focus more on protecting and growing those specific items.
  • Looking at the cohort data, customers tend to drop off significantly after their first purchase, which suggests the business is better at acquiring customers than retaining them.
Business Recommendations
Priority Finding Evidence (Real Data) Recommended Action
🔴 High Furniture is Losing Money The profit margin on Furniture is 2.5%, which means the business is actually losing money on every furniture sale. This is mainly because of how heavily it gets discounted. Look into the pricing and discount structure for Furniture. Reducing discounts and negotiating better supplier rates would directly improve the overall profit margin.
🔴 High Discounts Are Being Overused Orders with no discount bring in a 34.0% margin on average. But when discounts cross 31%, the margin falls to -91.5%. That gap is too large to ignore. Set a cap on how much discount can be applied without approval. Anything above 20% should need a sign off so the team stays aware of the margin impact.
🟡 Medium Monthly Sales Targets Are Being Missed Too Often Only 26 out of 48 months hit the 10% growth target. The average achievement was 108.5%, which is close but not quite there. It would help to do a quick mid month check to see if sales are on track. Catching the gap early gives the team time to push harder before the month ends.
🟡 Medium Central Region Needs Attention The Central region has the lowest profit margin at 7.9%, compared to West which is performing at 14.9%. It is worth looking at what is working in West and seeing if any of those practices can be applied in Central. Could be a product mix issue or a pricing issue.
🟢 Opportunity Technology Has the Best Margins and is Underused Technology runs at a 17.4% margin, which is the highest of all three categories. It is not getting the same attention as the other categories despite being the most profitable. Shifting some marketing focus toward Technology products makes sense. Bundling tech items or promoting them to the Corporate segment could bring a noticeable revenue boost.
🟢 Opportunity A Lot of Customers Are Going Quiet Around 21% of customers are in the At Risk segment, meaning they bought before but have not returned in a while. Only 4% are in the Champions group. A simple re-engagement email or discount offer targeted at At Risk customers could bring a good chunk of them back. Retaining existing customers is always cheaper than finding new ones.
